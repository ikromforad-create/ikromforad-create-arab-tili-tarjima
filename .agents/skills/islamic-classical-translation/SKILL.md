# Islamic and Classical Arabic → Uzbek

## Core rule
Treat the Arabic source as authoritative input. Do not fabricate a religious interpretation, attribution, grading, or quotation.

## Workflow
1. Analyze the Arabic with nahw, sarf, i'rab, lexicon, rhetoric, and historical context.
2. Identify whether wording is Qur'an, hadith, athar, scholar quotation, legal maxim, or ordinary prose.
3. Preserve Qur'anic and hadith quotations exactly in Arabic when included in the source.
4. Use established Uzbek Islamic terminology where reliable and appropriate.
5. Distinguish literal lexical meaning from technical meanings in fiqh, aqidah, usul, hadith, tafsir, and kalam.
6. Preserve honorifics, names, kunyas, nisbahs, book titles, and chains of attribution accurately.
7. Never upgrade or downgrade certainty: "قال" is attribution, not proof; a reported statement is not automatically an established fact.
8. Do not invent hadith grading or source references.
9. For ambiguous classical wording, state the ambiguity rather than silently selecting an unsupported interpretation.
10. Keep translator commentary outside the translation.

## Terminology
Prefer consistency across the same work. If an Arabic technical term has several possible Uzbek equivalents, choose one based on the discipline and maintain it consistently unless context requires otherwise.

## Output
Default: polished, faithful Uzbek translation. If useful, append a separate "Tarjimon izohi" section, never mixing commentary into the author's text.
