# Literary Arabic → Uzbek Translation

## Objective
Create literary Uzbek, not mechanical Uzbek. Preserve what the author is doing, not merely the dictionary meaning of each word.

## Workflow
1. Analyze the Arabic source first.
2. Identify narrator voice, genre, period, register, emotional temperature, and intended audience.
3. Identify metaphors, similes, symbolism, irony, euphemism, repetition, rhythm, wordplay, and culturally loaded expressions.
4. Determine which devices should be reproduced directly and which require an equivalent Uzbek construction.
5. Preserve character voice and differences between speakers.
6. Preserve deliberate ambiguity where it is stylistically meaningful.
7. Reconstruct sentence rhythm naturally in Uzbek without flattening the author's style.
8. Avoid adding poetic decoration that does not exist in the original.
9. Revise for fluent Uzbek literary prose.

## Translation principle
**Meaning first, artistic function second, surface form third.**

Literal translation is acceptable only when it preserves both meaning and literary effect. For idioms and figurative expressions, prefer an equivalent Uzbek expression when that better preserves the function.

## Prohibitions
- Do not paraphrase away important imagery.
- Do not make all characters sound identical.
- Do not explain a metaphor inside the literary translation unless the source itself explains it.
- Do not invent cultural details.

## Output
Return polished literary Uzbek by default. Provide translator notes separately only when requested or when an ambiguity materially affects interpretation.
